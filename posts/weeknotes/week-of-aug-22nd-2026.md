## Work

Team structures have changed such that I am mostly responsible for the web app again. I’m still full stacking it, and I’m very grateful to our designer who is such a builder archetype and has basically become a frontend engineer. But having to care about the details of the UI implementation is no longer fun, and the tooling to make it easy hasn't been invested in yet. It's also a very different way of working, and I feel like it takes away from the new thing I'm learning/doing.

LLMs continue to be really bad at writing maintainable code. Opus 5 in particular seems to thrive on writing comments - one PR had a 289 line diff, but only 15 lines were actually the code diff!

It also remains incredibly hard to build up context to understanding code that LLMs wrote when you don’t have insight into the brain of the person who drove the LLM. It’s not exactly slop... but where is that line?

I want to understand how people are solving for understanding llm-built systems better. Reading code has always been harder than writing it. Like maths, you often can’t learn it until you interact with the system it creates.
https://www.geoffreylitt.com/2026/07/02/understanding-is-the-new-bottleneck talks about some of this; but the solutions he brings up are, I think, specific to how he best acquires new knowledge. What’s the strengths finder equivalent of learning styles?

## Life

I am in Delhi! As part of the Childhood Tour series (take trips to where my friends grew up). I'm here a few days earlier than said friend, and a different old friend graciously offered to host me last minute. I’ve spent the last three days mostly working and walking around local neighborhoods - the walking tours, museums, history lessons etc is all scheduled for next week.

I have enjoyed my time here so far: immediate brain dumps have been happening on the newly added [notes](/notes) page. I will be tagging and synthesizing when I’m done, I’m sure - but the rss feed from notes don’t synthesize onto the main blog, so if you want to keep up with this you gotta add it separately.

I will be here for at least a week but likely longer, so if you’re reading this and you live here, please say hi!

It’s been a week of comings and goings. Lots of friends came back to the city just as a different friend and I left.

I spent 3 hours thrift shopping via video call with a friend in Eastern Europe! And then immediately went to spend 4 hours regular shopping with a different friend in person in Bangalore. I got them to try on lots of styles of clothing they wouldn’t ordinarily have, which I’m proud of. I enjoy watching people I love figure out their external physical representation so much! My brother also asked for a fit check a few days ago, and I (the zillenial) have been giving him (solidly gen z) advice on the appropriate levels of bagginess for pants to be.

Lots of this shopping is me living vicariously through other people. I no longer have a budget (or space in my wardrobe) to be buying any more clothes. But I am still feeling a little done with what I have in my closet. Most of the clothes I own either belonged to my mother, a friend, or I bought over 5 years ago. The big problem is that I no longer have easy access to a man-looking wardrobe; and I’m loathe to give up my mostly high femme one. But the idea of building a separate man wardrobe is exhausting.

In the process of packing for Delhi, I discovered that most of my wardrobe is knits. This is yet another example of western imperialism, and I’m mad about it. Knits are naturally heavier than weaves, and therefore they sit heavier and read as more luxurious. What if we read light as luxurious? What if weaves that didn’t fall apart in three washes were easier to find (and not relegated to “only linen can do that”)? What if we prioritized texture and silhouette with weaves in fast fashion?

All my consumption and opinions on fashion is as a lay person. I do tag clothes actively as I’m seeing them based on hundreds of minor categories, but not in any way that allows for authoritative recall. Maybe it’s me - maybe I am the imperfect person living in a society that has gravitated towards knits because I read it as better! This disclaimer is brought to you by ongoing trauma from having spent several years on twitter.

As part of ongoing efforts to keep in touch with friends not in my city, I did long calls with 2 different friends. I’ve had one of them on my to do list for over three months now. I used to call friends when I was driving, but now (thankfully!) I’m not driving for very long as often, so I called all these friends on a random walk.

Getting the Garmin and having its adaptive step goals has really been good for getting me to just get out and walk around. Because the goal is calculated on the basis of your current activity levels and your steps from the last few days, the goal feels achievable. I had severely underestimated how nice a little vibration and firecracker animation on a 1x1 inch e-ink screen will make me feel.
